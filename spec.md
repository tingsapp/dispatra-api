# Dispatra API specification

Updated: 2026-10-14. This is the target FastAPI/PostgreSQL contract. [Implementation state](state.md) records verified manual functionality and remaining automation work. `MUST` and `MUST NOT` are requirements.

## 1. Authority and scope

The API is the system of record for organizations, accounts, Shippers, Drivers, Vehicles, Orders, pricing, dispatch, Routes, POD, audit, and events. The client may preview but cannot authorize or finalize these decisions. Use FastAPI, Pydantic, SQLAlchemy/Alembic, PostgreSQL, Decimal money, and versioned OpenAPI. V1 is English-first Canadian local delivery. Payment collection, payroll, marketplace, warehouse, maintenance, and branch administration are outside the initial operational scope.

Dispatra presents itself as an AI-powered automatic dispatch system. AI may recommend, rank, explain, or summarize through authorized application services. Deterministic eligibility, state, tenant, price, capacity, and route rules always govern mutations; agent memory is not system state.

## 2. Vocabulary and lifecycle

- **Order:** Customer price unit, with one or more pickup and drop-off requirements. Lifecycle: `NEW → ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, or `CANCELLED`. Pricing result, attention flags and Route status are separate. Completed is the final successful Order status.
- **OrderStop / OrderItem:** Stable requested stops and linked physical items. Every item has a pickup and later delivery; multiple pickups/drop-offs and interleaved valid sequences are supported.
- **Route / RouteStop:** Optimized executable visit sequence, possibly containing multiple Orders. A Route binds one driver and one actual fleet vehicle through one active Assignment.
- **PricingSnapshot / ChargeLine:** Versioned, immutable priced result and its auditable parts. Completion and reassignment do not reprice it.
- **Shipper:** Ordering account and commercial defaults. A separately selected billing customer, stop recipient, and driver are different actors.

Risk, late start, pricing review, and failed attempts are attention flags or execution exceptions, not extra Order lifecycle values. Unknown data must remain unknown instead of becoming a fabricated status, coordinate, price, or GPS sample.

## 3. Architecture and tenancy

Use a modular API with application services for accounts, pricing, dispatch, routing, execution, POD, billing, events, and agent tools. Handlers authenticate, authorize, validate, invoke use cases, and return typed responses. A PostgreSQL organization UUID owns tenant records; company slug is a URL identifier, never authorization. Use a restricted runtime database role, forced row-level security, transaction-local tenant/customer context, composite tenant relationships, and cross-tenant non-disclosure. Public registration is limited to a new company and its dispatcher on a 30-day trial; platform owners, Shippers and Drivers cannot enroll through signup.

Retryable mutations use payload-bound idempotency keys, expected versions, audit, and a transactional outbox. Outbox consumers claim tenant-scoped leases, use the event ID for provider-side idempotency, and acknowledge or retry without holding a database transaction across external calls. Reusing a key with another payload fails. Background work reports accepted versus completed states accurately. Freeze migrations; do not edit an applied migration to change the contract. Provide least-privilege projections for Shipper and Driver portals.

## 4. Entities and invariants

Every mutable tenant record has a stable ID, organization scope, audit timestamps, and version. Directory deletion archives Rate Cards, Shippers, Drivers and Vehicles; it revokes affected login access and blocks retirement that would interrupt active Orders or Routes. Archived Shippers, Drivers and Vehicles disappear from ordinary lists, remain available for authorized historical reads, and cannot be edited through ordinary profile commands. Historical booking and pricing snapshots remain intact. Operational addresses, contacts, commercial choices, and pricing used by an Order are snapshots; later directory edits do not rewrite history. Preserve legacy fields for historical reads without putting removed controls back in new forms.

Company Profile settings accept a manually entered correspondence address or a retained structured address; operational Driver/Shipper/stop addresses remain structured. Company PNG/JPEG logos are bounded to 200 KB. Saving Company name also updates organization identity. Account email and role are read-only in the company Profile UI.

### 4.1 Order, Shipper, and commercial records

- **Shipper:** Required display name and email login; Business additionally requires Company name, Individual does not. One phone and one complete Warehouse Address; the address is the default pickup, not a separate Service Area setting. Store rate card, discount, instructions, account status, and legacy identities. Assign the customer number server-side. The single email is used for portal login, quotes, and delivery updates unless an explicitly versioned billing-payer model applies.
- **Shipper account:** Creating the Shipper and its shipper-scoped user occurs in one transaction. Shipper identity (number, name, contact, email, phone, status) and commercial profile live in one `shippers` record; legacy account-only records have no warehouse and are excluded from operational use. The account role is `SHIPPER`; any role rename requires a versioned migration. Generate an initial password and show it once; store only a secure hash. Enforce email login and nonblank phone uniqueness within the SHIPPER role across all companies; another role may share either contact. A Shipper sees only its own profile and authorized Orders.
- **Order:** Server-generated number; ordering Shipper or explicit prospect quote; service, rate card, scheduled/ready/deadline times, selected Accessorials, stop/item links, booking and payer snapshots, pricing status/snapshot, version, and lifecycle. Do not persist decorative list counters as truth. A Quote does not create an Order. Prospect quotes choose an active Rate Card without an existing Shipper. Authenticated Shippers can create and view their own Orders through the same booking/pricing service; their account identity and allowed commercial terms come from the session. Invalid or incomplete pricing remains explicit Needs Attention and blocks assignment. A booking may name an optional `preferred_driver_id` (a current active company Driver): a dispatch preference shown to the dispatcher, never an assignment, and it does not change the price. Every Shipper carries a Rate Card; a Shipper saved without a choice receives the company Default. When a distance card lacks distance or an hourly card lacks minutes, pricing obtains both from one Google Routes request over the Order's own stops (estimated minutes = driving time plus each stop's service minutes) and freezes them in the pricing context; Shipper projections never include that context.
- **Order history:** Customer and billing snapshots, saved prices, charge lines, and previous versions remain readable and unchanged after directory or pricing configuration edits. A permitted explicit reprice creates a new snapshot, never overwrites a final one.

### 4.2 Stops, cargo, and execution

Each OrderStop has stable ID, type, sequence, address/coordinates when resolved, contact snapshot, optional timing and instructions, and supplying pickup IDs. Each OrderItem has stable ID, positive quantity, physical weight/dimensions, handling unit, fragile/DG and other applicable handling tags, and explicit pickup/delivery IDs. Residential is an Accessorial choice, not a duplicate stop checkbox. No manual zone field is required; Zone pricing resolves from the configured address/postal mapping.

Validate every item link and pickup-before-delivery precedence. A failed or short pickup blocks only its dependent quantities. RouteStops preserve distinct visits even at the same address. Across the full proposed Route, physical load must remain between zero and actual vehicle capacity after every stop; cargo must fit the actual vehicle and required equipment. Unknown dimensions or qualifications cannot pass silently. Driver arrival, quantity confirmation and POD use stable revisions, assignment generations, and explicit actual loaded/unloaded quantities.

### 4.3 Driver, Vehicle, and telemetry

- **Driver:** Stable profile and least-privilege `DRIVER` account created transactionally. Name, phone, email login, and Canadian address are required. Derive the service city from the address; an edited address changes future coverage. Issue an organization-unique Driver number on the server, independent of the login email and row UUID. Preserve historical service-area IDs until a deliberate migration. Keep account, duty, work, app connectivity, GPS freshness, and location permission independent. On Duty means available for assignment; Off Duty blocks assignment. Starting duty does not request location permission or start tracking. A dispatcher Driver edit can open or close the duty session atomically with profile changes, with version checks and an audit record. It never grants location permission or starts GPS tracking. Store vehicle link and availability/qualifications. An assigned Order projection may include its immutable Shipper name, company name, phone and email snapshot needed for direct operational contact; it excludes the redundant warehouse profile address, commercial snapshots, payer and billing details, private notes and unrelated customer data. Drivers carry no employment type, payout shares or individual maximum active Orders override; the organization maximum applies to every driver.
- **Vehicle:** Actual fleet identity, plate/province, selected type, capacity/weight/volume/dimensions, equipment, optional positive Maximum stops, availability, and current driver/route links. Length, width, and height are required in new vehicle creation. Vehicle-specific pricing/capacity edits do not alter the shared type or another vehicle. Legacy surcharges/licence flags remain readable but are not new-form inputs.
- **Telemetry:** Capture time, server receipt time, accuracy, duty session, app last seen, and location permission. Connectivity derives from timestamps; missing/invalid/future telemetry is unknown. A dispatcher edit, duty toggle or push cannot start mobile GPS. The driver Start action requests location permission and starts sharing for route execution. Route finish stops sharing while leaving the driver On Duty for another assignment. Local End Duty stops sharing immediately, even offline. Dispatcher duty changes do not create telemetry.

A vehicle cannot be actively assigned to two drivers/Routes. Profile edits cannot release an in-use vehicle or alter loaded-goods custody; use an authorized assignment transition. Enforce organization-scoped unique numbers and plate/jurisdiction constraints.

## 5. Accounts and URL contract

The public site is `/`; platform owner administration is `/admin`; a dispatch company's Monitor is `/{company-slug}/`. Dispatcher pages live beneath it, including `/{company-slug}/shippers`. The authenticated Shipper portal is `/{company-slug}/shipper` and the Driver portal is `/{company-slug}/driver`; singular portal paths never collide with plural dispatcher pages. Older `/platform`, `/{slug}/shipper-portal`, `/{slug}/dispatch`, and `/{slug}/customer` client paths redirect. Company slugs cannot use the reserved top-level words in `RESERVED_SLUGS` (`app/schemas.py`), including `admin`, `api`, `prototype`, `login`, `signup`, `shipper`, `driver`, `pricing`, `about`, `help` and `support`.

The homepage company identifier opens `/signup?company={slug}`. `POST /api/v1/auth/signup` accepts company identifier/name, dispatcher full name, email, optional phone, optional company address (up to 500 characters) and password, with a payload-bound idempotency key. Save the trimmed address to company settings during provisioning; blank addresses preserve the legacy retry payload and existing records remain unchanged. It creates only a fresh tenant and one DISPATCHER, seeds the standard settings and catalogues, marks the company pending verification, hashes the password and issues only a restricted verification cookie. Queue a six-digit code through the platform SMTP sender; confirmation starts a 30-day trial and issues a normal session. Anonymous registration uses only its server-derived new organization scope, never platform RLS context or migration credentials. Roles, existing organization IDs and trial dates cannot be supplied. Reserved identifiers and per-role contact conflicts roll back all records; retry neither creates another company nor resets the trial, and a rotated password cannot authenticate an old signup replay. Browser Origin checks and signup rate limits apply. Passwords never appear in responses or saved operation results. No payment collection or initial password-change gate is part of signup.

Trial companies have readonly `trial_started_at`, `trial_ends_at` and `trial_activated_at`. The effective organization `active` property and matching SQL expression exclude unactivated trials at the exact cutoff, so HTTP sessions, API keys, WebSockets, TMS ingress, worker eligibility and owner Active/Suspended filters agree without a periodic expiry job. Sessions are capped at the trial cutoff. Established companies keep null trial dates and their existing access. At expiration the workspace is suspended until the platform owner activates it with the existing versioned activation command. Activation records its time, revokes old sessions and closes any old driver duty before restoring access; the original trial dates and operational history remain intact.

Platform owners manage the single monthly SaaS plan through `GET/PATCH /api/v1/platform/billing/plan`. The saved nullable monthly price uses Decimal/numeric(12,2), with an uppercase three-letter currency code. The price starts unset; CAD is the editable initial currency. Writes require the owner session, expected version and payload-bound idempotency key, and record a private audit event. Configuration endpoints remain owner-only. A SELECT-only public RLS policy permits reading the single global plan; owner context is still required for writes. Anonymous `GET /api/v1/subscription/plan` exposes only name, monthly interval, trial days, nullable price and currency, excluding internal ID, version and timestamps. The public plans page reads these saved terms; no payment/customer records are exposed. The plan reports a one-month free trial as 30 days, sharing the provisioning default. Price/currency edits do not alter company trial dates or access. Every company has one independent `company_subscriptions` record, initialized as NOT_SUBSCRIBED with a snapshot of the default monthly price/currency during provisioning and migration backfill. Owner-only `GET/PATCH /api/v1/platform/organizations/{id}/subscription` manages status (NOT_SUBSCRIBED, ACTIVE, PAUSED, CANCELLED), Decimal monthly price/currency and a nullable UTC calendar start date, with row locking, expected version, payload-bound idempotency and company-scoped `subscription.company_updated` audit. ACTIVE requires price and start date; zero-price agreements are allowed. Forced RLS allows only platform owners to read/update, while new-trial provisioning can insert its initial non-revenue record in its new tenant. Company roles cannot read the records or revenue. Subscription edits preserve company access and saved trials; workspace activation never infers a subscription/payment.

Owner-only `GET /api/v1/platform/billing/subscriptions` lists agreements and company/trial/access metadata with escaped search, status filter and UUID cursor pagination. Saved `monthly_price` values in the default plan and company agreements represent price per vehicle per month. `GET /api/v1/platform/billing/revenue` aggregates all saved agreements independently of pagination, using exact Decimal sums per currency, monthly recurring revenue and monthly × 12 annualized projection. Each contributing company's amount is its saved rate multiplied by its current non-archived registered fleet vehicle count, including inactive vehicles; zero vehicles contribute zero. Count fleet Vehicle records, never catalogue Vehicle Types, drivers, assignments or browser stores. Aggregate vehicle counts by organization before joining agreements to preserve subscription/status counts and avoid cross-company multiplication. Amounts, fleets and subscription counts share one SQL statement snapshot. Only explicitly ACTIVE agreements whose UTC start date has arrived contribute; future agreements are scheduled. Report total/active/scheduled/not subscribed/paused/cancelled counts. Unconverted trials, paused/cancelled/unsubscribed companies generate no recurring revenue. Workspace suspension does not cancel the independent commercial agreement. Existing stored prices remain unchanged and acquire the per-vehicle meaning; no migration or automatic repricing occurs. No currency conversion or collected-revenue claim is made. Recurring charges, payment providers and subscription invoices remain unconfigured. Delivery invoicing remains outside V1.

Users sign in with role, email and password, without selecting a company. Both web and native driver login resolve the company from the role/email account match. Login IDs are case-insensitive and unique within each role across companies, including inactive and archived accounts; different roles may share the same email. Nonblank account phones are also reserved per role, ignoring formatting and an optional Canadian country prefix. Driver/Shipper account phones follow their directory records; the current dispatcher account phone follows Company Profile phone. Blank optional phones reserve nothing. Database constraints arbitrate concurrent writes, and profile/directory/import/settings writes synchronize the phone reserve transactionally. A SELECT-only, transaction-local login policy finds the one role/email account before normal organization RLS applies; it grants no write access or other company records. Optional legacy organization hints must match the resolved account. Existing duplicate contacts require explicit reconciliation before migration; never silently rename, delete or merge accounts.

The platform owner provisions each company with one dispatcher account; public trial signup creates the same company/dispatcher/default-catalogue records atomically. Additional dispatcher creation is rejected, including when the existing account is inactive. Only one dispatcher can be active per company; historic extra accounts remain inactive and cannot be activated alongside it. New companies receive five active Rate Card methods: Distance based, Fixed per delivery, Zone to zone, Hourly and Imported price. Imported price is not the company default and adds no surcharges. Existing custom or archived imported cards are preserved by additive seeding and backfill. Exactly one `ADMIN` exists; it is created and recovered only by the interactive `app.bootstrap` command with migration credentials, never through HTTP or with default credentials. Owner sessions use the same cookie, hashing, expiry, rate-limit, origin and no-store rules; they cannot use company endpoints or impersonate a company account. Company creation accepts optional phone (up to 50 characters) and correspondence address (up to 500 characters). Owner company list and detail expose `logo_url` from existing Company Profile settings; list projection reads only this settings field with a scoped outer join, retaining companies without settings and preserving search/pagination. Owner company detail exposes phone/address from the existing company settings and its `settings_version`. Rename/Overview commands may edit either contact field with that settings version; omitted fields preserve saved data, blank values clear them, and an unchanged address text preserves its structured value. Both admin and dispatcher Profile share this source and reject stale settings drafts. Contact/name/account/access/password writes remain atomic, audited and idempotent; omitted new fields preserve historical request hashes and older replay results are enriched without reapplying writes. Company detail exposes `shipper_count`, `driver_count` and `order_count` from saved records scoped to that company, across all statuses including archived directory records. Existing login-account/session counts remain compatible. Older idempotent company results are enriched with these additive totals without reapplying the command. Platform endpoints list/search, inspect and rename companies (the slug is immutable), suspend or activate them, show administration audit history, and create, edit, activate/deactivate, reset and sign out dispatcher accounts. Every mutation checks the record version, is idempotent and audited. `PATCH /platform/organizations/{id}/overview` saves company name, dispatcher login/display name and company active status in one transaction, checking company and dispatcher versions and reusing the existing identity, audit, suspension/session and duty rules. The nested dispatcher input accepts an optional `new_password` (12–128 characters, without trimming); the owner supplies it directly, it is Argon2-hashed in the same transaction, all dispatcher sessions are revoked, and `dispatcher.password_changed` is audited. It never appears in responses, stored operation results or audit fields. A validation, login-conflict or stale-version failure leaves every field and password unchanged. Generated dispatcher passwords are returned once and never stored in replay results or audit. The last active dispatcher of a company cannot be deactivated. Suspension revokes every company session and open driver duty and blocks company login (a correct password receives an explicit suspension response); activation does not restore sessions. Company history, Orders and audit records are never deleted by these commands. Driver/Shipper email is the login ID, with a generated one-time initial password. Use Argon2 hashes, opaque hash-stored sessions, expiry/revocation, attempt limits, exact-origin mutation checks, secure cookies, no-store responses, and redacted credential handling. Do not force a first-login password change unless the product decision changes. Password reset revokes old sessions. Customer profile writes cannot change role, tenant, commercial terms, or identity outside allowed fields.

## 6. Pricing authority

Owner company creation and public trial signup accept optional strict-boolean `tms_workspace`, default false, meaning External TMS when true and Dispatra TMS when false. The UI's **Use Dispatra TMS** checkbox is checked for false. False/omitted creation requests retain older idempotency payload shapes; true is payload-bound. Shared provisioning saves this choice and, for External TMS, selects the existing imported-price Rate Card as company Default before initial login. Dispatcher AccountView login/me/verification projections derive it from their own settings; other roles report false. Missing legacy values default false. Older settings updates omitting the preference preserve its saved value and retry payload shape. Explicit versioned settings saves switch rates and preference within one company-locked transaction: remember the active calculated Default in the private, company-scoped `company_settings.native_rate_card_id`, select imported pricing for External TMS without renaming an existing card, and restore that calculated card for Dispatra TMS. If the saved calculated card is no longer active, use the active existing STD fallback; reject rather than invent rates if neither is available. Imported pricing retains the supplied total/tax/reference; default imported cards take precedence for TMS intake. Rate changes emit normal company events and refresh clients. Existing order pricing/snapshots and other settings remain unchanged. Migration 0048 adds only the nullable restore reference with a composite tenant FK; no backfill changes existing rates. Signup verification and trial timing are unchanged. This choice alters navigation/default pricing, never identity, authorization, connection state or vendor certification.

The latest simplified [web pricing surface](../client/spec.md#6-pricing-and-company-profile) is the new-quote contract. Preserve older saved cards and frozen quotations through versioned compatibility. Use Decimal/numeric monetary values and explicit cent rounding. Company units are display/edit conventions; canonical physical values remain consistent.

Active new Rate Cards use `BASE_PLUS_DISTANCE`, `FIXED`, `ZONE`, or `HOURLY`; existing `IMPORTED` cards remain supported for historical/edit flows. Resolve an explicit permitted Order card, then the Shipper's attached card, then the organization Default; ties or missing required rates produce a review error, never an arbitrary price. A Zone card uses a single central pickup, destination-zone columns, and weight From/To bands. A new card starts with 0–99 lb and $20 for Zone 1. The configured zone is resolved from the destination address/postal mapping; no matching price means Needs Attention. Chargeable Zone weight uses the greater of actual and dimensional weight where configured. Base + Distance does not charge weight; package weight still constrains actual vehicle fit.

Service Level is a fixed additional charge, Vehicle and Accessorial charges are configured separately, Fuel Surcharge is a percentage of eligible positive charges, and the Shipper owns its discount. A Rate Card owns its minimum subtotal. Ordinary delivery pricing does not charge routine travel time; Hourly contracts and explicit recorded waiting are separate. Imported final agreed totals bypass new modifiers according to their frozen terms.

Fragile and DG are catalogue Accessorials with editable rates, initially $15 and $25 per package unit. A new booking selects them from the item `fragile` and `dangerous_goods` flags and multiplies each rate by the sum of flagged item quantities. The price context records this rule so older frozen contexts retain their former flat Accessorial terms. Other Accessorials continue to charge once per selected Order.

### 6.1 Formula and tax

For a new quote, calculate method-specific freight, then permitted service, vehicle, fuel, Accessorial, and adjustment lines, less discount. Apply the card minimum to the resulting subtotal before tax:

```text
subtotal = max(minimum, freight + service + vehicle + fuel + accessorials + adjustments - discounts)
tax = GST/HST taxable base × enabled GST/HST rate
    + provincial taxable base × enabled provincial rate
total = subtotal + tax
```

Each tax is a separate ChargeLine. The optional GST/HST registration number identifies the company tax account; it does not silently toggle either tax. Disabled rates do not charge. Explicitly flagged special/imported/exemption conflicts require tax review before assignment. Freeze tax rules, rates, and taxable bases in the quote snapshot. Historical destination/profile tax decisions remain unchanged until an allowed explicit reprice.

### 6.2 Snapshots and locks

Freeze card ID/version, formula inputs, method, charge lines, discounts, minimum, tax, subtotal, total, and relevant booking context in a versioned PricingSnapshot. Rate changes never mutate an existing quote or saved Order price. Reassignment, merged-route distance, deadhead, driver changes, and completion do not change the customer price. Keep customer-priced distance separate from actual operational route distance and internal cost.

## 7. Dispatch and eligibility

There are exactly `AUTO` and `MANUAL` modes. AUTO evaluates a priced, ready Order and assigns a feasible driver and actual vehicle; MANUAL leaves it unassigned until dispatcher selection. After ranking, the worker rechecks AUTO under the same company lock as settings writes before saving an assignment or no-candidate decision. Both modes apply identical hard checks and automatically optimize afterward. No feasible candidate creates a specific Needs Attention reason. The company maximum active Orders limit applies to every driver in both modes.

Check tenant ownership, active accounts, driver duty/work state, address-derived pickup service city, vehicle type/equipment/capacity, qualifications, shift/availability, maximum work time/stops, Order windows, quote validity, item links, physical load after each stop, route state, and custody. Any dangerous-goods item requires the driver's `DG` qualification in both AUTO candidate evaluation and manual assignment. A text skill alone does not prove a two-person crew. AI may rank feasible candidates, but cannot assign in MANUAL or waive hard checks. Preserve reasons, scores, chosen candidate, mode, actor, versions, and audit.

## 8. Routes and driver execution

Optimize multiple Orders with pickup/drop-off precedence, capacity, time windows, service duration, locked visits, distance, and ETA. Return stable RouteStops with planned loads and revision. Do not silently mutate locked/in-progress Routes or insert into active Routes without an explicit safe command. The Driver app receives only its own assignment and allowed actions. Route start needs online validation; authorized work on a started cached Route may continue offline. Failed pickup dependencies and exception history remain explicit.

## 9. POD and completion

Resolve server POD policy per delivery visit. Receiver present requires name and signature, plus a photo when policy requires one. Unattended delivery requires permission, safe placement, and photo; otherwise record a failed attempt. A client `NONE` request cannot waive server evidence. Protect evidence through offline retry and accept it with tenant/version/idempotency checks before completion.

Valid completion persists Completed and the completion time while retaining the booked pricing snapshot. Completion never issues an invoice, changes the quoted price, requires hourly billing review or queues financial email. POD and unresolved operational issues still govern completion. A priced, unexpired prospect Quote can be sent to an explicitly entered recipient. Quote and delivery-update emails have independent pending, sent, failed and uncertain states; uncertain SMTP acceptance never triggers automatic resend. V1 has no invoice issuance, invoice documents, invoice endpoints, receivables or payment collection. Migration 0028 privately archives existing invoice records and email requests, retires their pending sends, and maps existing Invoiced Orders to Completed without changing saved prices or evidence.

## 10. Agentic workflows, events, and audit

**Order agent (email intake).** Each company may connect one IMAP mailbox; its app password is stored only encrypted and never returned, and internal mailbox hosts are refused. New messages are read read-only from the mailbox's current end and stored once per Message-ID as tenant-scoped, dispatcher-only `email_intakes`. The AI step only extracts facts into a fixed schema and treats the email as untrusted data; units, addresses (precise geocode whose postal code matches), service choice (the named service, else the company settings `default_service_id`) and `Booking` validity are decided deterministically. An Order is created automatically only for a complete booking from an active Shipper's email address whose receiving provider reports DMARC or aligned DKIM pass, through the shared Order service as that Shipper's own account with actor type `AGENT` and source `EMAIL`. Emails that do not ask for a new pickup or delivery (stories, newsletters, replies, notifications and similar) are recorded as not an order and ignored: no draft and no notification. Every other order email is held for dispatcher review as a draft with its missing facts (an unknown sender's draft has no Shipper and lists it as missing), or as failed when it could not be read. Dispatchers may complete the draft (source `EMAIL`), link a Shipper (which vouches for the sender and re-runs the agent), re-run, or discard. Agent outcomes notify company dispatchers; an agent-created Order also notifies its Shipper.

The manual services and transactional events are built first. In the later automation phase, agents may extract emailed bookings, recommend feasible assignments, explain exceptions, summarize operations, and invoke approved application commands under the actor's permissions. They never write tables directly or bypass idempotency, tenant scope, pricing, capacity, locks, POD, or audit. Provider failure does not corrupt committed state. Outbox events notify clients and workers; clients recover missed, duplicate, or out-of-order events from authoritative snapshots/versions.

Every audited company mutation also appends an `events` row in the same transaction, whether a user, a worker (`SYSTEM`) or a later agent (`AGENT`) caused it. Each event carries type, actor, entity/version, Order/Route references, correlation/causation identifiers and an audience: company dispatchers, one Shipper, one driver and/or one recipient account. Account, credential and platform-administration actions are not fanned out to the company event feed. A separate owner-only administrative inbox reuses recipient-scoped notification rows: dispatcher settings saves and dispatcher password changes create informational notices; reported operational issues, disconnected order mailboxes and unknown quote-email acceptance create critical notices. All notices are transactional and generic, without addresses, contact values, passwords, issue descriptions or operational references. Owner actions do not notify the acting owner; system failures still notify the owner when that owner initiated the underlying operation. Unknown acceptance of ordinary order-update email does not create a critical owner notice. Repeated failed mailbox polls create one notice until recovery; a new failure episode advances the mailbox version so both owner and dispatcher alerts can be stored again. `GET /platform/notifications` returns newest-first owner notices and its unread total, with optional before/limit paging; versioned/idempotent `POST /platform/notifications/{id}/read` and idempotent `POST /platform/notifications/read-all` affect only that owner’s rows. Company roles cannot access these endpoints or read owner notices through their tenant inboxes. The owner-ID lookup uses neutral account context; the notice write uses its trusted company scope and then restores all caller scope, including driver/Shipper filters. Pending caller writes are flushed before this internal context switch. No schema migration or company impersonation is needed. Events contain identifiers only; clients refetch affected records through authorized reads.

`GET /companies/{slug}/sync?cursor=` is the single client feed for dispatchers, Shippers and drivers. Without a cursor it returns the current position; with one it returns up to 200 changes visible to the caller, the next cursor, the caller's unread notification count, `reset` (malformed or foreign cursor: reload everything) and a server-chosen `next_poll_ms` (`SYNC_POLL_MS`, default 15000; 0 when more changes are waiting). Cursors order by writing transaction and sequence and expose only events older than every in-flight transaction, so later-committing lower sequences are never skipped. Server consumers keep independent positions in `event_subscriptions`. The HTTP feed remains a polling fallback. On long-running hosts, authenticated `WS /companies/{slug}/ws?cursor=` streams the same `SyncView` contract, with a 15-second heartbeat, replay from the last processed cursor and session/tenant/audience revalidation on every read. Browser sessions require an exact allowed Origin; native driver sessions use the existing bearer header, never a token in the URL. The stream accepts no mutation commands. Each connected stream reads the shared PostgreSQL event log every second by default (`WEBSOCKET_SYNC_SECONDS`, bounded 0.25–5 seconds), so separate API processes and workers share the durable feed without process-local broadcasts. GPS uploads emit identifier-only driver and active-Order tracking events; coordinates remain behind authorized tracking projections.

The rules in `app/events/rules.py` create per-account `notifications` from events in the same transaction, never for the acting user: Shipper bookings, edits and cancellations, price review, delivery, completion holds, reported issues and failed/uncertain email for dispatchers; assignment, start, delivery, unassignment, dispatch edits/cancellations for the Shipper; assignment, route release and dispatcher duty changes for the driver. `GET /notifications` (newest first, `before` paging), versioned idempotent `POST /notifications/{id}/read` and `POST /notifications/read-all` serve every role; each read change emits a recipient-only event so other sessions update. Text contains Order numbers but no address, contact, price or private notes.

## 11. REST/OpenAPI contract

Expose versioned `/api/v1` typed resources, cursor pagination, structured errors with request IDs and field details, expected versions, and idempotency keys. Generate client types from OpenAPI. Role-specific endpoints return minimum necessary fields; a hidden UI control is not authorization. ## Public entity IDs

## 14. Connected TMS (2026-10-10)

One external TMS per company connects with no TMS development: the TMS owner creates an API key and, for webhooks, enters Dispatra's URL and signing secret. All tables (`tms_connections`, `tms_links`, `tms_inbox`) are company-dispatcher scope under forced RLS; Shipper and driver sessions never read them.

**Connection.** `GET/PUT /companies/{slug}/integrations/tms` (dispatcher) holds: enabled, HTTPS base URL (no credentials, query or fragment; hosts must resolve to public addresses), API key (`X-API-Key`) or bearer token, drivers/vehicles/orders endpoint paths, status endpoint containing `{id}` (the TMS order ID), fleet sync interval (15/60/360/1440 minutes), order method (webhook or polling at 1/5/15 minutes) and field maps. The credential and webhook signing secret are Fernet-encrypted with the server key, required on first use, kept when omitted and never returned; the view reports `credential_configured`, `webhook_secret_configured` and the required fields still unmapped per record. Saves are versioned and idempotent. `POST .../tms/test` reads the saved drivers endpoint with the saved credential and imports nothing. TMS calls never follow redirects, cap time and size, and log short error codes only.

**Field maps.** `app/integrations/fields.py` is authoritative for keys and required fields; unknown keys or malformed paths are refused. Weights and dimensions are read in the company's unit settings and converted for storage.

**Fleet sync.** On the interval and on Sync now, Dispatra reads the drivers and vehicles endpoints, maps each record and creates or updates drivers and vehicles through the normal directory use cases with a `SYSTEM` actor, linked by TMS ID in `tms_links`. New drivers get a generated-password login like any created driver; dispatchers hand it out with Reset password. **Order intake.** `POST /companies/{slug}/integrations/tms/events` accepts any JSON order payload signed with HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body using the secret (`X-Signature`, hex), without session, Origin or idempotency headers. Valid payloads are stored in `tms_inbox` and acknowledged with 202 before processing; repeats with the same TMS order ID and payload hash are ignored. Polling stores the orders endpoint's records the same way. Processing maps the order, matches the shipper by TMS shipper ID then email or creates it (first pickup as warehouse when no address is mapped), matches the service and vehicle type by code or name, and creates, updates or cancels the order with source `IMPORT`, the TMS order ID as `external_reference` and the company's active Imported price rate card with the mapped total and tax. Items link to stops by stop ID; IDs may be omitted only for one pickup and one drop-off. Anything unmapped or invalid leaves the payload in `NEEDS_REVIEW` with reasons. Only `NEW` orders are updated; later changes are held for review.

**Status back.** A server consumer (`tms-status`) of the company event log, created at the first save, sends each order event (`order.created`, `order.updated`, `order.cancelled`, `order.assigned`, `order.unassigned`, `order.started`, `stop.arrived`, `stop.completed`, `evidence.accepted`, `issue.reported`, `issue.resolved`, `order.completion_held`, `order.completed`) for TMS-linked orders as `POST` to the status endpoint, in order, with the event ID as `Idempotency-Key`, retrying a failure from the same event without skipping. Dispatcher completions emit the same `stop.completed`/`order.completed` events.

**Company webhook.** `GET/PUT /companies/{slug}/integrations/webhooks` holds one HTTPS destination (public hosts only) and an encrypted signing secret (required first, never returned). Consumer `api-webhooks` sends the same order events for every order, signed with HMAC-SHA256 of the JSON body in `X-Signature`, with the same ordering and retry rules. `python -m app.integrations.worker` runs fleet sync, polling, intake processing and both deliveries.

**Dispatra API keys.** `GET/POST /companies/{slug}/integrations/api-key` (dispatcher session) shows the company's key prefix, creation and last use, or creates a new 256-bit `dsp_` key, revoking the previous one at once; the key is returned only in the create response and stored as a SHA-256 hash. `Authorization: Bearer <key>` (no session cookie, Origin or `X-Requested-With`) acts as the company with dispatcher permissions on the documented paths only — orders (list, create, get, update, cancel, tracking, proof of delivery), shippers/drivers/vehicles (list, create, get, update, archive) and `GET /catalog` — and every other path returns 403.
